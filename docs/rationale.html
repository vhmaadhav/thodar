Thodar

Design rationale and evidence: what we built, why each choice was made, and how it works
Doctors set the plan. Thodar makes sure it happens.
Team Highest in the Room · Dr Sughapriya V. C (paediatrician, clinical lead) · Maadhav V H (team and technical lead) · Sachin A S · Nakul T · Nishanth R S
Live demo: thodar.onrender.com (synthetic data) · October 2026

1. In one paragraph

Thodar (Tamil for "to continue, to follow through") is a follow-up desk for hospital and clinic care teams. It ties mother and baby into one care thread, from the first antenatal (ANC) visit to the child's second birthday. It reads the registers clinics already keep, links records into one thread per mother–baby pair, turns doctor-set plans and national schedules into due dates, and gives nurses a daily list of who is due, overdue or unreachable. Families get one WhatsApp or voice reminder in Tamil or English for the clinic's next session day; their replies, including voice notes, update the list. Thodar is assistive, not clinical: it never diagnoses, scores risk, interprets results or gives advice.

2. The problem: families start care, then drift

95.9% → 65.2%start antenatal care → complete 4+ ANC visits (India)1
>96% → 87.1%any vaccine → fully immunised, children 12–23 months1
71.8%receive the second measles dose1

Even Tamil Nadu, among India's best performers, moved backwards between NFHS-5 and NFHS-6: four-visit ANC fell from 90.6% to 87.6%, full vaccination from 90.4% to 89.7%, and urban mothers (86.2%) now do worse than rural (88.6%).2 Urban care runs through hospitals and private clinics, which is where Thodar works.

Why it happens. Pregnancy to age two is more than a dozen scheduled contacts across two departments. At delivery the mother's record stays with obstetrics and the baby's begins in paediatrics, so no one owns the whole journey. Follow-up in facilities still runs on registers, spreadsheets and phone calls, and much of the loss is preventable: in an Indian study the top reasons for a missed vaccine were no reminder (32.9%) and forgetting (26.6%).3 Even vaccinated children are often late: 34.8% of measles doses were delayed.4

What already exists. The government's JANANI platform (May 2026) gives field nurses (ANMs/VHNs) due lists and longitudinal records;5 its main risk is adoption and the data-entry burden on those nurses.6 Kilkari and mMitra send one-way voice messages to mothers. None gives a hospital or clinic team a single mother–baby thread with two-way replies, which is the gap Thodar fills, beside these systems rather than replacing them.

4. Staying in scope: assistive, not clinical

The brief rules out diagnosis, treatment recommendations, clinical decision support, clinical risk scoring, interpretation of medical data and autonomous advice. Thodar's rules are built around that:

  • Fixed rules decide everything clinical-adjacent. Due dates, order and ownership come from versioned schedule files a clinician signs off. The worklist is sorted only by days overdue and failed contact attempts, never by a risk score.
  • AI only reads paper and talks to families. It digitises registers (a nurse confirms every row) and transcribes or labels replies with scheduling labels only (confirm, reschedule, moved, wrong number, stop).
  • Two locks on safety. Every typed or spoken reply is read by rules and a language model. If either sees a health concern, a nurse handles it and the family is told a person will call (or to dial 108). The model can escalate but never overrule a rule. This was added after live testing found a Tanglish message ("my daughter is a bit unwell, can we come next week") that the rules alone read as a reschedule.
  • DPDP Act. Consent is recorded before reminders and can be withdrawn; a family's data can be exported and erased; every change is in an audit trail; data is meant to stay in India.

5. How it works

1 · ReadRegisters, Excel, HIS exports; photos of paper pages via Sarvam Vision, checked by a nurse.
2 · LinkRecords matched on PICME/RCH ID, ABHA, phone, name and dates; unsure matches go to review.
3 · ScheduleANC, PNC and UIP rules + doctor-set intervals become dated visits.
4 · WorklistDue, overdue, unreachable; one-tap actions; handover panel.
5 · ReachOne WhatsApp/voice reminder per family; replies update the list.

7. Pilot plan (90 days) and limits

Pilot at our paediatrician's practice, measured against a baseline from the same clinic's registers. Headline measure: families brought back into care (overdue visits that happen after Thodar follows up), which the prototype already counts. Also: ANC visits on time, PNC at 48 h and day 7, vaccine doses on time from 6 weeks to 9 months, and staff hours a week spent on follow-up calls.

Honest limits. Schedules, catch-up periods and Tamil wording need clinical sign-off before any pilot. WhatsApp needs a verified business number; voice calls through Sarvam Voice Agents are designed but not yet placed live. Some sources below were read as summaries only (marked *).
